# Speak the selected poker language

Write directly for the target audience, using actual player/coach/commentator usage. Selecting a language does not require translating every familiar poker term. A brief first-use explanation can help beginners; experienced viewers usually prefer familiar shorthand. Preserve meaning and consistency without a word whitelist or mandatory intro formula.

| Concept | Mainland Chinese examples | English | Japanese |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Committing the stack | all-in、全下、直接推 | all-in, shove, jam | オールイン |
| Best possible hand | 坚果、坚果牌、nuts | the nuts | ナッツ |
| Raise level | 3bet、三 bet、再加注 | three-bet | 3ベット、スリーベット |
| Continuation bet | cbet、持续下注 | c-bet | Cベット |
| Position and response | 按钮位、check back、抓 bluff | button, check back, bluff-catch | ボタン、チェックバック、ブラフキャッチ |

These are choices, not automatic string replacements. A nut flush can lose to a full house on a paired board; a nut draw is not a made nut hand. A 3bet is not necessarily a raise to three times the previous amount. Check the hand and action before choosing the phrase.

Keep the speaker's point of view coherent: what I hold, what I know now, what I want a bet to accomplish, and how I will respond. In a multi-street lesson, carry earlier actions into the next decision. Highlight the important inference and give it room to land; avoid reciting every definition or narrating opponents' hidden thoughts as facts.

Choose visible notation and spoken pronunciation separately. IndexTTS pronunciation replacements can expand a rank, acronym or loanword without changing captions; check the actual result. Emotion and rhythm should follow the reasoning, not alternate mechanically between neutral sentences and exaggerated excitement. Do not claim human delivery, expert endorsement or retention from a successful render or ASR transcript.
